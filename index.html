About the Bridge

A shared language for climate information integrity.

Climate evidence is complex. AI can help people understand it — but it can also reproduce misleading narratives, omit important context or make uncertain conclusions sound definitive.

ClimateSignal brings together the people who produce climate evidence, develop AI systems, fact-check claims and communicate scientific findings. The goal is to turn an urgent challenge into a shared research agenda with common concepts, evaluation methods and lasting collaborations.

Unlike a paper-centred workshop, the Bridge combines accessible disciplinary primers, a guided claim-auditing lab and collaborative studios. Participants learn together in the morning and build practical research directions together in the afternoon.

Research agenda

Questions we want to tackle.

The Bridge centres on challenges that need both climate-science expertise and AI or information-integrity perspectives.

01 / REPRESENTATION - Claims, context & uncertainty

How do we represent climate claims together with their geographic and temporal scope, provenance, evidence and causal attribution?

02 / VALIDATION - Grounding in evolving science

How should AI retrieval and fact-checking systems handle changing evidence, scientific sources, projections and generated summaries?

03 / ROBUSTNESS - Multimodal mis- and disinformation

How can we evaluate misleading plots, synthetic media, selective baselines, outdated findings and adversarial framing?

04 / COMMUNICATION - Human-centred explanations

How can AI communicate limitations, calibrated uncertainty and useful context without further amplifying misleading claims?

Participation

Bring your perspective to the table.

The Bridge Event is designed for climate and AI researchers, fact-checkers, social scientists, communication and policy specialists, practitioners, and students at different career stages. No specialist knowledge of every field is expected.

Participants will be invited to share short non-archival position statements, climate-claim cases or demonstrator abstracts. The aim is constructive cross-disciplinary exchange, not a collection of finished papers.

Participation instructions and any Bridge-specific deadlines are not yet confirmed.

At a glance
  • Submission Requirements: We welcome short (up to two-page) non-archival position statements, case descriptions, preliminary research findings or demonstrator abstracts rather than requiring mature research results
  • Selection of participants is intended to balance expertise, motivation and access for early-career researchers.
  • Guided lab supports both programming and non-programming participants.
  • Submitted work will be presented via posters.
  • Submissions will be through OpenReview, more details TBD
  • Attendance and registration follow AAAI rules.
